# Killer B's Bottle Tracker — changelog

Newest first. The file header in index.html carries the headlines; the full entries live here.

## v0.1.27  ·  2026-08-31 10:44 PM ET

Logo recoloured, type filter compacted, and the layout adapts to a desktop. The mark was black line art on a solid white square, so it read as a white box against the parchment page; it is now the app's own ink on a transparent ground, with the anti-aliased edges preserved by using luminance as the alpha rather than a hard cut. The home-screen icons keep a parchment ground on purpose, because iOS puts them on a wallpaper where a transparent mark would disappear. An audit check now fails the build if the mark ever loses its alpha channel, verified against a deliberately flattened copy. The twelve type chips wrapped two and three rows deep and pushed the shelf itself off screen; they are now one pick list that opens over the list rather than displacing it, shows a tick and a count per row, reads All types or the single name or the number chosen when shut, and closes on any tap outside it. On a wide screen the single phone column was stretching to the full window, which made every row a long thin ribbon and the summary tiles enormous: the measure is now capped, the tiles run six across, the summary charts sit two across so the dashboard fits one screen instead of six, and the nav bar is centred rather than spanning the whole width.

## v0.1.26  ·  2026-08-31 10:42 PM ET

Screen actions moved to the top on MadGolf's pattern, and the shelf gained an importer. Every list screen now carries its actions in the header beside the title — Add bottle and Import on the shelf, Build a flight on Flights, Spin and Clear holds on Time for a Taste — and both detail screens put their buttons in a row directly under Back, so the thing you came to do is not below the record you came to read. Import takes a CSV from Only Drams, a spreadsheet of your own, or an export from this app: column names are matched loosely across about fifty aliases, so Name or Bottle or Product all read as the name and ABV or Proof or Strength all read as the strength. Only Drams exports ABV rather than proof, so anything under 60 in a strength column is read as an ABV and doubled — 43 becomes 86, 57.5 becomes 115. Categories match loose spellings and a region names its country, so Islay reads as Scotch and Single Pot Still as Irish; an unrecognised category falls back to bourbon and says so on the row rather than filing it silently. Nothing is written until you have seen the preview, which counts what would be added, what is already on the shelf and would gain a sealed spare, what repeats inside the file, and what has no usable proof, and lists the rows worth a second look with their line numbers. A downloadable template and a column specification sit in the same dialogue, and the template round-trips through its own importer with no issues, which is the only test that makes it a template. One real bug found on the way: a row repeated inside the file was being overwritten as already-owned, so re-importing an export with a duplicate line would have added two sealed spares for one bottle. +51 tests.

## v0.1.25  ·  2026-08-31 10:37 PM ET

Data QA became a gate, and every US distillery is now on the map. validate.py checks the catalog against itself and against the rules of the categories — a bottled-in-bond that is not 100 proof, a name stating 12 years against a stored 10, a Scotch below 80 proof, a region on something that is not Scotch, a wine verdict with no finish, a duplicate name, a bottle pointing at a product that is not there, and the stocking rule of exactly one open per whisky — and it runs inside ship.py, which now refuses to ship on any data error. It found a real 44-bottle gap on the first run: a known finish with no wine verdict, so Ardbeg Uigeadail and forty-three others were neither wine nor wood. All are classified now, taking wine-cask to 97 and confirmed wood-only from 7 to 13, and no finish is left unclassified. Warnings fell from 50 to 6, of which four are correct filings the checker flags conservatively and two are secondary prices worth your eye. On the map, all 53 US distilleries carry real coordinates and draw as pins over the state shading — Kentucky and Tennessee at the same granularity as Islay, so Bardstown's seven distilleries and Louisville's six read as the clusters they are. Zoom ceilings are now per layer because one number cannot resolve a map 4.5 degrees wide and one 35.6 wide: Scotland 80x for the Islay trio, the United States 900x for Bardstown, and both verified to zero overlapping pins at their ceiling. Brands sharing a site fan onto a small ring so neither pin is unreachable, deterministically and never more than a mile from the truth. +45 tests.

## v0.1.24  ·  2026-08-31 10:30 PM ET

Tasting-note provenance corrected, and history is now visible. I wrote the flight cards, colour and nose and palate and finish included, so the 187 notes lifted off them are prompts written to guide a taster — not the distiller's notes, and not sourced. They now say so wherever they appear rather than posing as fact, and a test forbids any card note from claiming a producer as its origin. Notes carry one of three origins: written for a card, the producer's own, or your own tasting, and you can now enter or correct all four columns yourself on any bottle, saved as yours. The Info panel reports how much of the shelf is described and how much of that is only prompts. lookup.gs ships alongside the app: an Apps Script backend holding your API key server-side that serves the Add a bottle lookup and, run from the editor, batch-sources tasting notes for the bottles that have none into a CSV on your Drive for review before anything is merged — the app copies the missing names for you. Time for a Taste gained a history below the machine, tabbed between Pours and Flights, each with a six-month bar of activity, a tappable log back to the bottle or flight, and a way to remove a mistaken entry. One real bug found by a precomputed expectation: the log stamped its row index after filtering by kind, so removing an entry would have deleted a different one from the history. +27 tests.

## v0.1.23  ·  2026-08-31 10:25 PM ET

Six changes from on-device use. Shop moved next to Shelf in the tab bar, and Taste and Flights now carry line-art Glencairns — one glass and three — instead of geometric glyphs. Completion is confirmed and dated rather than assumed: tapping a glass on Time for a Taste opens the bottle with its notes and an explicit I poured this, and a flight is marked run through a dialogue that asks when, defaulting to today but accepting any past date, because a flight gets written up the morning after as often as the night of. The date is validated properly — the 31st of February and a future date are both refused rather than silently stored. Flights gained the same browsing the shelf has: search across title, variable, premise and the names of the pours; filters for not run, run and fully pourable, plus type chips counting flights rather than bottles; and sorts for curriculum order, name, most ready, lowest proof and recently run, with a never-run flight sorting last rather than posing as the oldest. Pours on the proof ladder are now tappable and open that bottle. And the notes you asked for turned out to already exist: the flight cards carry colour, nose, palate and finish for all 221 core pours, so those are lifted onto the catalog and 187 bottles now show the distiller's notes in sheet order on their own panel, credited to the card they came from. +41 tests.

## v0.1.22  ·  2026-08-31 10:19 PM ET

The shelf gained sorting and six more filters. Sort by name, proof either direction, distillery, oldest, dearest or cheapest — and a missing value sorts last on every key rather than reading as zero, so a no-age-stated bottle never poses as the youngest and an unpriced one never leads the cheapest list. Ties break on name so the order never depends on insertion. A More filters panel opens under the existing search, status and type rows and adds proof band, recognition, release, occasion, Scotch region, cask and age, every chip carrying how many bottles it would show. Cask and age are one-of-three rather than multi-select because wine cask, wood only and no finish are mutually exclusive states — and a bottle whose wine status is unknown counts as neither, rather than being swept into wood only. All facets AND together, the button shows how many are on, and there is a Clear all. The empty state now says how many filters produced it instead of a generic line. +36 tests.

## v0.1.21  ·  2026-08-31 10:16 PM ET

Time for a Taste can no longer land on a dead spin. The reels used to roll blind and could stop on a combination nothing on the shelf satisfies, leaving three empty glasses; a spin now picks a pourable bottle that satisfies whatever reels are held and describes it with the ones that are not, each unheld reel either narrowing to that bottle or opening up, so at least one pour always stands. Holds that rule out everything return nothing at all rather than spinning, and the machine names which holds are impossible so you know which to release. Verified by driving 400 spins over the real 325-bottle shelf and 400 more over the fixture: no empty paylines, holds always honoured, and 94 percent of spins fill all three glasses. Type on the machine was too small to read at arm's length: reel labels, faces, the hold marker, the Spin button and the glass captions all went up, the reel value area is taller so two-word faces like American Malt sit on two lines rather than clipping, and the Glencairns are larger. +9 tests.

## v0.1.20  ·  2026-08-31 10:14 PM ET

Map pins were unusable: sized in pixels while the SVG works in degrees, so a single-bottle pin drew at 64 percent of Scotland's width and the dots covered the country. Pin radius is now a fraction of the map's own span, scaled between a floor and a ceiling against the largest count in that layer, so one bottle reads at 4.3 percent of the width and twelve at 9 percent, and the same function works on a map 4.5 degrees wide and one 203 degrees wide. Label and count type sizes are map units for the same reason. A second, deeper fault fixed alongside it: pins shrank by the square root of the zoom while the view shrinks by the zoom itself, so pins grew on screen as you zoomed in and a cluster could never come apart however far you pushed. They now hold a constant screen size. The zoom ceiling went from 40x to 80x because Ardbeg, Lagavulin and Laphroaig only separate above about 31x, and a ceiling of 40 left no room past the one view the map exists for. Overlapping pin pairs across Scotland fall from 30 at full extent to none at maximum zoom. Ten new tests pin the geometry, including that no pin exceeds a tenth of the map on the real data and that the Islay trio overlaps when zoomed out and separates at the ceiling.

## v0.1.17  ·  2026-08-31

Installable with auto-updates, on MadGolf's pattern. The page checks for a new service worker on every load rather than waiting out the browser's 24-hour delay, checks again hourly, and hands the waiting worker a SKIP_WAITING message the moment nothing is half-finished on screen — the analogue of MadGolf's mid-round guard here is an open modal or a form field with typing in it. When it is busy it toasts and polls every twenty seconds instead of leaving the update stranded until the next cold start, then reloads once on controllerchange. Home carries an Install button where Chrome offers one and iOS instructions where it does not, since Safari never fires beforeinstallprompt; both disappear once the app is running standalone. Manifest completed with id, scope, description, orientation, categories and a maskable icon. Caching split by what it holds: the shell is cache-first because it only changes on a bump, while data.json and map.json are network-first with the cache as fallback — this app is used at home and in shops rather than on the back nine, so a current shelf beats an offline one. Fault tolerance where it actually bites: localStorage is probed once at startup so private browsing degrades to a session rather than throwing, each saved key is validated for shape and discarded individually if unreadable so one corrupt entry cannot take the shelf down, a failed save says storage is full instead of silently losing an edit, a failed data load explains what to check, and an unhandled render error toasts rather than leaving a blank screen. Eight new audit checks cover the update handshake, the install hook, the maskable icon and the storage probe.
